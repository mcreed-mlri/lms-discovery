# Design help for the Learning Hub: what to ask CviaD for

**The ask, in one line:** use the budget (a few thousand dollars) for two pieces of
research we cannot do well ourselves: testing how the binders are organised, then
watching real advocates use the redesign. CviaD is already making the
organisation's name and logo, so this brief only adds what the Learning Hub needs
from that kit.

Status: draft for approval. Prototype content throughout the hub is unreviewed.

## Why research, not more design

The screens, layout and code are moving quickly in-house: mockups take an
afternoon and cost nothing extra. What we cannot produce ourselves is evidence
from the people who will use the hub, gathered by someone who did not build it.
Outside help is worth the most there.

## 1. Test how the binders are organised (do this first)

Everything in the hub depends on staff finding things through its structure:
binders, then the tabs inside them, then topics. We invented that structure
(Practice Foundations, Litigation, Beyond the Courtroom; tabs such as Case Prep,
Trial and Post-Trial). It has never been checked against how advocates think
about their work, and it gets more expensive to change with every course filed
in it.

**What CviaD would do**

- **Tree test** (online, 15 to 30 staff, done in their own time). People see only
  the structure, written as a list of names with no design, and answer
  questions such as "Where would you look for how to answer a hearsay
  objection?" by clicking where they would go.
- **Card sort, if the budget allows.** Advocates group 30 to 40 real topics in
  whatever way makes sense to them, which shows the categories they would
  naturally create.

**What we get back**

- Which binders, tabs and names send people to the wrong place, and how often.
- A recommended structure, with the specific names and placements to change.
- An answer to one open question: should the Curriculum Map sit under Faculty
  Support, or somewhere attorneys can see it?

## 2. Usability sessions with five advocates (after the tree test)

The team comparison tells us what staff prefer. Watching new attorneys and
paralegals use the hub tells us where they actually get stuck.

**What CviaD would do**

- Five one-on-one sessions of about 45 minutes, on the participants' own phones,
  using the redesign preview with the corrected structure from step 1.
- The six tasks from the team comparison guide: find your place, browse to
  hearsay, search for it, practice one skill, read a course part and cross into
  practice, and get back.
- One task the preview cannot show on its own: opening a course from the
  Brightspace app on a phone and moving into the hub's practice. This needs a
  sandbox course.

**What we get back**

- A short findings report, ranked by severity.
- Clips of the moments people struggled.

If the budget comes in nearer $1,500, run three sessions and keep the tree test.

## Runner-up: a course author's design guide

If the content team will build many courses soon, a guide is worth considering
instead of the card sort: how to write and lay out a course part in the new
template, what belongs in the reading, how long a part should be, and when to
send learners to the hub for practice. Every course built afterwards follows it.

## What to ask for in the brand kit CviaD is already making

So the new identity works in the hub without a second round of work:

- A mark that stays legible at small sizes (a browser tab, a 28 px header badge).
- An app icon in the shapes phones need, including the "maskable" version with
  safe space around the mark.
- A version for dark mode.
- How "Learning Hub" sits beside the new organisation name.
- Files as SVG, with the organisation owning them.

When the name changes, "MLRI" needs replacing across the hub and the course
packages, so we need the final name before launch.

## What not to spend it on

- **More screen mockups.** We produce and revise those in-house.
- **A general UX audit.** It costs about the same as testing and tells us less.
- **New colours.** The current colours have checked contrast and are locked for
  accessibility (ADR 0008). Any new design should use them.
- **Code or a design system rebuild.** That work is under way.

## What we hand CviaD at the start

So the budget goes on new work, not catching up:

- The redesign preview link, with demo sign-in.
- The team comparison guide (tasks and verdict table).
- The mockup canvas comparing today's course page with the proposed one.
- `DESIGN.md` and `docs/learning-design.md` from the hub repo.
- The current binder structure as a list, for the tree test.

## How to contract it

- A written brief with fixed deliverables, not open hours.
- Order: tree test, then sessions, so the sessions test the corrected structure.
- Confirm that we own all research outputs and brand files.
