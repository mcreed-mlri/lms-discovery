Defending against a hearsay objection in writing

What to know before you answer the objection: what makes a statement hearsay, and the five steps of a written response.

By the end of this part you can

  • say why a statement has been challenged as hearsay;
  • tell whether an exclusion or exception may apply;
  • explain in writing why the evidence should be admitted.

What hearsay is

Hearsay generally involves an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of what the statement asserts.

The same words, two purposes

The same words may be hearsay for one purpose and not hearsay for another, so your written response should name the purpose clearly.

If the statement is not offered for its truth, the objection may fail at the threshold. If it is hearsay, the response may need an exclusion or an exception.

Exclusions and exceptions

Exclusions and exceptions are rule-specific routes to admission. This part names the analysis move without supplying jurisdiction-specific doctrine.

Key legal authorities (the controlling rules, cases and practice notes) will be added after review by a legal subject-matter expert.

The five steps of a written response

  1. Identify the challenged statement.
  2. State the purpose for which the evidence is offered.
  3. Identify the applicable exclusion or exception.
  4. Connect the facts to the rule.
  5. Request admission clearly.

A drafting frame

"The challenged statement is offered to ________. It is not hearsay because ________. Alternatively, it is admissible under ________ because ________."
