5 Review the Procedure

Build the Written Response

  1. Identify the challenged statement. Quote or describe it precisely.
  2. State the purpose for which the evidence is offered. Do not skip this step.
  3. Identify the applicable exclusion or exception. Use reviewed authority in a production course.
  4. Connect the relevant facts to the rule. Show why this record fits.
  5. State clearly why the evidence should be admitted. End with the request.
"The challenged statement is offered to ________. It is not hearsay because ________. Alternatively, it is admissible under ________ because ________."

Reference

Key Concepts

What hearsay is

Hearsay generally involves an out-of-court statement offered to prove the truth of what the statement asserts.

Hearsay vs. non-hearsay

The same words may be hearsay for one purpose and non-hearsay for another. The written response should name the purpose clearly.

Why the distinction matters

If the statement is not offered for its truth, the objection may fail at the threshold. If it is hearsay, the response may need an exclusion or exception.

Exclusions and exceptions

Exclusions and exceptions are rule-specific routes to admission. This prototype names the analysis move without supplying jurisdiction-specific doctrine.
